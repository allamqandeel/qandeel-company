## Purpose
Establish a provider-independent company performance, reporting, evaluation, and learning system that measures qualified outcomes rather than raw activity, supports role-specific performance profiles, attributes failures causally, converts validated failures into durable learning, and gives the Founder concise exception-first visibility.

## Canonical Founder Decisions

1. No single universal employee score. Each persistent employee has a role-specific, multi-dimensional Performance Profile. Hard gates may apply for critical requirements, but QANDEEL does not rank all employees on one simplistic company-wide score.

2. Failure and success require causal attribution. QANDEEL distinguishes Employee Judgment, Model, Tool, Context Retrieval, Workflow, Provider, Requirement, and External Dependency causes. Employee identity and performance are not conflated with model/runtime failures.

3. QANDEEL may automatically apply bounded quality protections when evidence is strong, including reduced autonomous scope, Quality Hold, skill suspension, increased review requirements, and re-certification. It may not autonomously promote, demote, or terminate a persistent employee based only on performance scoring.

4. Continuous learning is governed. Experience Memory, observations, candidate lessons, and current-work knowledge may update continuously. Skill proficiency, workflow preferences, routing behavior, and skill updates require evidence/evaluation. Identity, foundational personality, authority, risk limits, mandatory skills, certification requirements, and company rules are not self-modifiable.

5. Real failures become durable learning through a governed path: Real Failure → Cause Analysis → Valid Failure Case → Regression Candidate → Review/Calibration → Permanent Regression or Gold Case. Hidden/holdout cases are preserved to reduce overfitting to the evaluation suite.

6. Founder reporting is exception-first and conversational: concise Daily Company Brief, Weekly Operating Review, Monthly People & Capability Review, plus on-demand natural-language inspection of company, department, employee, or work-item performance.

## Canonical Architecture Contracts

- QANDEEL owns its evaluation contracts, evaluation data, scorecard definitions, learning records, and reporting semantics. No model provider or third-party evaluation service is canonical authority.
- Evaluation is provider-independent and combines offline evals, production outcomes, trace/process evidence, review results, and validated real-world failures.
- Task completion is not equivalent to review success or outcome verification.
- Quality is prioritized over raw activity. Message count, task count, token count, and tool-call count are observability metrics, not standalone performance measures.
- Scorecards are role-specific and multi-dimensional. Dimensions may include quality, reliability, cost efficiency, speed, collaboration, judgment, improvement, and role-specific outcome measures.
- Minimum-sample and uncertainty rules apply before interpreting performance trends or applying consequential changes.
- Review Pool outputs are themselves calibrated and monitored for false approvals, false rejections, drift, and uncertainty.
- Cost analysis uses cost-per-qualified-outcome and distinguishes productive cost from retries, fallbacks, escalation overhead, and failures.
- Learning signals may update Academy cases, skill-gap detection, re-certification needs, routing preferences, and workflow improvements through governed processes.
- Company, department, employee, and work-item reporting are linked but not collapsed into one universal metric.
- Founder-facing reporting surfaces material changes, exceptions, decisions, repeated failure patterns, capability gaps, quality drift, and economically meaningful trends rather than metric overload.

## Scope Boundary

Stage 17 DOES include:
- reporting semantics and cadence;
- role-specific performance profiles;
- evaluation architecture;
- causal attribution;
- quality and learning loops;
- regression/Gold Case governance;
- skill-gap and re-certification signals;
- cost-per-qualified-outcome analysis;
- Founder-facing performance and learning summaries.

Stage 17 DOES NOT include:
- production implementation of dashboards or reports;
- final UI visual design;
- unrestricted autonomous HR actions;
- provider-specific evaluation lock-in;
- final pilot validation thresholds, which belong to Stage 18;
- Environment Readiness execution, which remains mandatory before implementation.

## Final Gap Review

No material design gap remains that blocks Stage 17 closure. Remaining validation work belongs to implementation and Stage 18 controlled pilot, where scorecards, review calibration, cost accounting, learning effects, and reporting usefulness are proven against real workloads.
