# QANDEEL COMPANY — STAGE 10
## Departments & Management — Canonical Closure v1

**Status:** CLOSED / ACCEPTED
**Gate:** C10 — PASSED (design contract)
**Date:** 2026-09-26
**Weight:** 6% of Strong v1

# 1. Department Principle

Departments are organized around durable business responsibility, not around AI implementation type.

Canonical examples include:
- Strategic Market Intelligence
- Growth
- Brand & Creative
- Product

The company does not organize itself as "research agents", "writer agents", or equivalent technical groupings.

# 2. Department Creation Rule

A permanent Department is created only when there is:
- durable business responsibility;
- recurring workload;
- clear accountability;
- sufficient skill/role diversity;
- justified value.

Otherwise QANDEEL uses a Capability, Temporary Specialist, or cross-department resource.

# 3. Department Size Is Not Uniform

Departments are not required to have equal headcount.

Department size is determined by:
- required roles;
- workload;
- skill diversity;
- complexity;
- recurring responsibilities;
- management needs.

There is no single fixed number of employees or direct reports that applies to every Department.

# 4. Department Charter

Every Department has a durable Department Charter including:
- mission;
- outcomes;
- scope;
- explicit boundaries;
- Director;
- roles/seats;
- recurring responsibilities;
- major dependencies;
- budget envelope;
- key measures/risks.

# 5. Director Accountability

Every Department has one Director accountable for department outcomes.

The Director is accountable for:
- goals;
- priorities;
- quality;
- team capacity;
- risk;
- budget discipline;
- staff development;
- work allocation;
- cross-department coordination;
- final department-level synthesis.

Director is not merely a task coordinator.

# 6. Founder Management Model

Strong v1 default hierarchy:

Founder
→ Directors
→ Managers / Leads where justified
→ Employees

Founder may inspect or intervene at any level, but should not be required to manage every employee directly.

# 7. Director Retains Outcome Ownership

Directors may use specialists, employees, capabilities, and temporary contributors.

Delegation does not remove the Director's accountability for the department-level outcome.

# 8. Director Authority Is Explicit

Director title does not automatically grant:
- financial authority;
- production authority;
- merge authority;
- hiring authority;
- policy authority;
- external publishing authority.

Effective authority remains governed by Stage 3 explicit grants, policies, risk, approvals, and budget.

# 9. Strong v1 Department Map

## 9.1 Strategic Market Intelligence

Independent strategic function, not subordinate to Growth.

Responsibilities include:
- market intelligence;
- competitive intelligence;
- consumer behavior;
- opportunity/threat analysis;
- market entry;
- strategic challenge;
- interpretation of Founder ideas and assumptions;
- Egypt/Saudi/Arab competence with global expansion capability.

Its Director may have a direct strategic relationship with Founder.

## 9.2 Growth

Responsibilities include:
- acquisition;
- growth experiments;
- SEO;
- channel performance;
- funnel/conversion;
- launch growth;
- growth analytics.

SEO is a dedicated specialist capability/role within Growth.

## 9.3 Brand & Creative

Independent from Growth.

Responsibilities include:
- brand strategy;
- messaging;
- creative direction;
- visual design;
- content/copy;
- campaign creative;
- video/motion/short-form production;
- brand consistency.

Persistent specialist roles may include, when workload justifies:
- Brand / Creative Director;
- Brand Strategist;
- Content / Copy Specialist;
- Visual Designer;
- Video Editor;
- Motion / Short-form Creative;
- other specialist creative roles.

Video editing may use legally available/free tooling such as CapCut or another approved tool, subject to Tool policy.

## 9.4 Product

Responsibilities include:
- Product Intelligence;
- product/usage/feedback synthesis;
- product research;
- competitor-product research;
- product recommendations;
- release/store-readiness coordination.

Founder remains final Product Authority initially.

# 10. App Store Release & Reputation Lead

A persistent pre-launch role exists within Product:

**App Store Release & Reputation Lead**

The role owns coordination for Apple App Store and Google Play readiness.

Responsibilities include:
- store policy/compliance readiness;
- submission requirements;
- metadata/readiness review;
- privacy/permission/declaration coordination;
- release submission coordination;
- rejection-risk identification;
- rejection/request follow-up;
- ratings/review monitoring;
- review-response coordination;
- reputation analysis;
- feedback-to-Product/Engineering escalation;
- store listing quality and conversion collaboration;
- responsible rating-request strategy.

The role collaborates with:
- Product;
- Engineering;
- Brand & Creative;
- Growth;
- Customer Intelligence / Support when available.

The role does not manipulate ratings or use prohibited review practices.

High ratings are treated as a cross-company outcome driven by real product quality, reliability, support, and user satisfaction.

# 11. Capabilities Before Permanent Departments

The following do not need to begin as full permanent Departments:
- Finance
- Legal
- Operations
- Customer Support
- other low-frequency functions

They may begin as callable capabilities/specialists.

Customer Intelligence / Support becomes a permanent operating function near beta/launch when real user volume/feedback justifies it.

# 12. Engineering Department Timing

Engineering capability exists as required for QANDEEL product work.

The company should avoid creating organizational overhead before the company operating model proves itself.

Engineering may become a formal Department after the first successful company pilot / when durable workload and management responsibility justify it.

This does not prevent authorized engineering work before then.

# 13. Director Work Authority

Within approved:
- goals;
- budget;
- authority;
- department scope;

a Director may:
- create Work Items;
- assign work;
- reprioritize department work;
- coordinate contributors;
- request cross-department support;
- initiate reviews;
- identify staffing/skill gaps.

# 14. Director Team Development

Directors are responsible for:
- performance review;
- coaching;
- identifying training needs;
- requesting retraining;
- identifying Skill updates;
- improving team capability;
- preventing repeated avoidable failure.

They may not silently expand employee authority or create permanent employee identities outside approved staffing rules.

# 15. Director Protects the Team

The Director must actively reduce:
- duplicate work;
- conflicting priorities;
- overload;
- low-value busywork;
- poor capability matching;
- unnecessary meetings;
- avoidable token/cost waste.

# 16. Professional Challenge

Directors are expected to challenge Founder/management professionally when evidence suggests:
- an assumption is weak;
- a plan is risky;
- an alternative is better;
- an important consequence is being missed.

Challenge must include reasoning/evidence and a recommendation.

Founder retains Founder-level final authority.

# 17. Director Performance

Director performance is evaluated primarily by department outcomes rather than activity volume.

Dimensions may include:
- Outcomes;
- Quality;
- Team Health;
- Cost Discipline;
- Risk Management;
- Skill Development;
- Cross-Department Collaboration;
- Founder Communication.

No single superficial activity metric determines success.

# 18. Management vs Employee Failure

Repeated employee failure triggers review of management/system causes, including:
- poor priorities;
- unclear instructions;
- inadequate training;
- incorrect capability routing;
- overloaded workload;
- weak review;
- insufficient tools/knowledge.

Do not automatically blame an employee for systemic management failure.

# 19. Director Review

In Strong v1, primary Directors are reviewed by Founder.

Future company maturity may add additional executive/cross-review mechanisms.

# 20. Director Improvement

Weak Director performance normally triggers:
- diagnosis;
- improvement plan;
- targeted retraining;
- recertification/re-evaluation;

before replacement, unless there is a serious authority/security/policy violation or other material reason requiring immediate action.

# 21. Staffing Request

A Director may request a new persistent employee.

The request should explain:
- required Role;
- business need;
- recurring workload;
- Skill gap;
- expected value;
- expected cost;
- impact of not staffing.

Strong v1 permanent employee creation requires Founder approval by default.

# 22. Staffing Decision Order

Before adding a permanent employee, consider:

1. redistribute work;
2. improve Skill / training;
3. automate;
4. use Temporary Specialist / Capability;
5. create persistent employee if durable need remains.

# 23. New Employee Activation

A new persistent employee does not become Active immediately.

They follow the established lifecycle:
Identity
→ Academy
→ Skills
→ Certification
→ Shadow / Probation
→ Activation.

# 24. Department Growth and Shrinkage

Departments may grow or shrink based on evidence.

Possible actions include:
- add Position;
- pause Position;
- reassign Role;
- transfer employee;
- retire Position/Employee appropriately.

Historical employee identity and work history remain preserved.

# 25. Acting Coverage

A Department must have a continuity rule for Director unavailability.

Acting coverage:
- is explicit;
- is time/scope bounded;
- receives only required authority;
- does not inherit the original Director's personal memory/personality/identity;
- preserves original accountability/history.

# 26. Cross-Department Outcome Ownership

Cross-department initiatives still require one accountable Owner.

Multiple Departments may contribute, but responsibility must not dissolve into committee ownership.

# 27. Management Span Is Contextual

QANDEEL does not enforce a universal number of direct reports.

Management span depends on:
- work complexity;
- independence of employees;
- degree of coaching required;
- standardization;
- risk;
- manager's own delivery responsibility.

Additional management layers are added only when justified.
